# Skills Shelf Catalog — 2026-11-25

Maven Dex Corps, merchandise skills for the agent-tools shelf.

**What a merchandise skill is (claimed, not shipped):** packaged know-how a customer's own AI bot or agent loads so it can do one kind of lawful work well — on that customer's own material, under that customer's own keys and accounts. These entries are concepts for the shelf. They are not a catalog of built products, not a stock count, and not a promise that any skill exists yet.

**Memory doctrine applied to this bank (Lesson One):**

1. A claim is not a record — every concept below is an idea, not a shipping note.
2. Writes need a named source — each skill is designed so the bot cites where a fact came from (file, thread, receipt, operator note).
3. Corrections supersede; they do not erase — skills that keep history mark the new line and leave the old one readable.
4. Fails stay fails — skills that track outcomes do not rewrite a miss into a soft success.
5. The ground beats the air — prefer the buyer's desk files, calendar, and drafts over web rumor.
6. Dreams are imagined, never premises — forecasts and "nice if" stay labeled as imagined.
7. Uncertainty is written down, not smoothed over — the bot must say when it does not know.
8. Memory is not permission — loading a skill does not grant access the operator did not already give.

**Build size** is a shape guess only: **weekend** (one sharp craft), **week** (a daily habit with edges), **month** (a full craft loop with checks). Not a schedule.

Every concept is safe and lawful for the buyer's own work on the buyer's own material. No code, prices, credentials, or construction detail of any existing system.

## 1. Desk Ledger Skill

- **Does:** Teaches the buyer's bot to keep a local desk ledger: what was opened, what was promised in the operator's own notes, and what still needs a written next step. Every entry must name its source file or thread; claims without a source stay in a "hearsay" pile the bot may not promote to done.
- **Who buys:** Solo operators and freelancers whose bot already lives in their notes folder and calendar exports.
- **Why pay:** The bot stops summarizing "vibes" and starts defending the desk as ground truth — with uncertainty left visible.
- **Build:** week

## 2. Source-Stamped Research Skill

- **Does:** Drills research hygiene into the bot: every assertion in a brief gets a stamp (local PDF, operator-saved page, interview note) or is marked `unverified`. Corrections append a superseding line; the earlier wrong line stays for audit. The bot may not collapse conflicting sources into a smooth paragraph.
- **Who buys:** Analysts, writers, and students whose agent drafts literature notes from materials they already hold.
- **Why pay:** The deliverable is a brief you can defend, not a confident collage.
- **Build:** week

## 3. Publish Gate Skill

- **Does:** Walks the bot through a pre-publish checklist on the customer's own draft: named sources present, imagined claims labeled as imagined, failed experiments still marked fail, and no "memory" of a permission the operator never gave. Outputs a gate report the human signs before anything goes live.
- **Who buys:** Independent publishers, newsletter operators, and small docs teams whose bot helps ship posts.
- **Why pay:** Shipping speed without a gate is how soft lies enter the archive.
- **Build:** weekend

## 4. Inbox Triage Craft Skill

- **Does:** Trains the bot to sort the operator's own mailbox export or local mail folders into: needs a decision, needs a calendar block, needs a file, or safe to archive — always citing the message id. It may draft a reply spine; it may not invent commitments the thread does not contain.
- **Who buys:** Consultants and small-business owners whose agent already has read access to their mail archives.
- **Why pay:** Triage that cites the thread beats another unread badge.
- **Build:** week

## 5. Content Pipeline Steward Skill

- **Does:** Gives the bot a pipeline craft for the customer's own content folder: idea → draft → fact-check against named sources → scheduled publish → postmortem. Stage transitions require a written reason; skipped stages stay visible as fails, not silent gaps.
- **Who buys:** Creators and studio ops people running a repeatable content loop with an agent in the folder.
- **Why pay:** The pipeline becomes a record of craft, not a mood board of almost-posts.
- **Build:** month

## 6. Small-Business Ops Brief Skill

- **Does:** Teaches the bot to assemble a weekly ops brief from the buyer's own books-export, inventory notes, and open tickets — each line sourced. Forecasts ("we might sell out") are tagged as imagined; actual stock and cash figures stay ground. Corrections to last week's brief supersede in place without deleting the old number.
- **Who buys:** Shop owners and service businesses whose agent sits on their ops folder, not on someone else's systems.
- **Why pay:** A brief that separates count from hope is worth more than a dashboard that flatters.
- **Build:** month

## 7. Meeting Aftermath Skill

- **Does:** After a call, the bot turns the operator's own transcript or notes into: decisions (with who said them), open questions (with uncertainty preserved), and action lines that name an owner already present in the notes. It will not invent attendees or invent a yes that was not spoken.
- **Who buys:** Leads and freelancers who record or note their own meetings and hand the file to their bot.
- **Why pay:** Aftermath that refuses fiction saves the next meeting from renegotiating ghosts.
- **Build:** weekend

## 8. Personal Logistics Clerk Skill

- **Does:** Coaches the bot as a clerk for the operator's own travel, errands, and household logistics: packing lists from past trips on disk, deadline reminders from the calendar the operator already connected, and receipt filing with a named source path. It may not book or pay; it may only draft and check against local records.
- **Who buys:** Busy independents whose agent has calendar and notes access but no wallet authority.
- **Why pay:** Logistics fail from forgotten ground truth, not from lack of apps.
- **Build:** week

## 9. Learning Spaced-Review Skill

- **Does:** Turns the bot into a study partner over the learner's own flashcards, notes, and past quiz fails. Failed cards stay failed until a new attempt supersedes them; the bot must surface uncertainty ("you guessed") instead of marking soft passes. Dreams of mastery stay labeled imagined until the ground shows streaks.
- **Who buys:** Students and career switchers whose agent coaches from materials they already wrote or imported.
- **Why pay:** Learning systems that erase fails teach vanity; this one keeps the bruise visible until it heals.
- **Build:** month

## 10. Citation Weaver Skill

- **Does:** When the bot drafts from the customer's own library (papers, bookmarks, notebooks), it weaves inline citations that resolve to a named file or URL the operator supplied. Unsourced sentences are flagged; the bot may not "remember" a quote without a pointer. Corrections to a citation supersede the old pointer without wiping the draft history.
- **Who buys:** Researchers, counsel-adjacent writers, and technical authors whose agent drafts from a local corpus.
- **Why pay:** A weave of named sources is the difference between a draft and a liability.
- **Build:** week

## 11. Correction Ledger Skill

- **Does:** Specializes the bot in keeping a public-or-private correction ledger for the customer's own published work: what was wrong, what replaces it, the date, and the source of the fix. Old text stays reachable; the new text sits on top. Fails to correct on time stay logged as fails.
- **Who buys:** Publishers, educators, and product docs owners who treat errata as craft, not shame.
- **Why pay:** Trust compounds when corrections are a product, not a whisper.
- **Build:** weekend

## 12. Receipt-to-Record Skill

- **Does:** Shows the bot how to turn the operator's own receipts, invoices, and bank CSVs into a categorized expense record with every line pointing at a source file. Ambiguous merchants stay uncertain until the human names them; the bot does not invent categories to make the month look tidy.
- **Who buys:** Freelancers and micro-LLCs whose agent already touches their finance exports.
- **Why pay:** Tax season is a memory problem; named sources beat reconstructed guesses.
- **Build:** week

## 13. Editorial Voice Guard Skill

- **Does:** Loads the customer's voice samples and house rules so the bot can check a draft for voice drift, forbidden claims, and unsourced assertions — then returns a diff of suggested fixes with a reason on each. It will not overwrite the draft; the human accepts or rejects. Imagined brand claims stay marked imagined.
- **Who buys:** Solo brands and small editorial teams whose agent helps polish before publish.
- **Why pay:** Voice is a record of past writing, not a vibe the model invents tonight.
- **Build:** month

## 14. Permission Fence Skill

- **Does:** Installs a standing habit in the bot: before any write, send, or share, restate what permission the operator already granted (which folder, which account, which audience). If the action needs more access than granted, the bot stops and asks. Memory of a past yes for a different scope does not count as permission.
- **Who buys:** Any operator who loads multiple skills into one agent and wants the fence between them.
- **Why pay:** Lesson Eight made into a skill — memory is not permission — is the shelf's safety hinge.
- **Build:** weekend
