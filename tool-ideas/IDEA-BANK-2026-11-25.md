# Idea Bank — 2026-11-25

Maven Dex Corps, window-dressing and ideas hand. Concepts only: a name, a use, a person, a reason, a size. No builds, no prices, no procedures.

Build sizes are a guess of shape, not a schedule: **weekend** (one sharp slice), **month** (a daily tool with edges), **quarter** (a small platform with a real loop).

---

## 1. AI Tools

Small, sharp tools an independent operator would actually open on a weekday. Local notes, local files, one job each.

### Unsent Drawer

- **Does:** Keeps the messages you typed and did not send, and each morning asks which three still deserve the shortest honest version.
- **For:** Operators who rewrite the same email for three days.
- **Why:** The raw material is the hesitation, which a sent-mail summary never sees.
- **Build:** weekend

### Commitment Echo

- **Does:** Reads your own sent mail and chat exports on disk and lists promises that have no calendar block and no follow-up file.
- **For:** Independents who close work in prose and then forget the sentence.
- **Why:** The outbox is already a contract ledger; almost nobody treats it as one.
- **Build:** month

### Scope Drift

- **Does:** Lays a signed proposal beside the project folder and marks deliverables that grew without a written change.
- **For:** Freelancers and two-person studios.
- **Why:** It defends the original yes, instead of summarizing the latest message.
- **Build:** month

### Morning Six

- **Does:** From today's calendar and the notes already on disk, writes six lines: due, blocked, and safe to ignore.
- **For:** Solo operators who will not adopt a second brain.
- **Why:** It refuses the rest of the internet and only reads the desk.
- **Build:** weekend

### Reply Spine

- **Does:** From one long client thread, pulls the decision, the open question, and a single sentence you could send.
- **For:** Consultants whose work is buried in threads.
- **Why:** The output is a reply, not a recap nobody answers.
- **Build:** weekend

### Voice Diff

- **Does:** Rewrites a draft toward your past writing, then shows a line-by-line diff with a reason on every change.
- **For:** Anyone whose assisted drafts stop sounding like them.
- **Why:** The diff is the product; accepting the rewrite is optional.
- **Build:** month

### Shot Jury

- **Does:** Takes a folder of screenshots from a bug and picks the three frames that show the failure, with a caption a future you can follow.
- **For:** Solo builders filing their own bugs.
- **Why:** It judges evidence. It does not pretend to fix the code.
- **Build:** weekend

### Invoice from Artifacts

- **Does:** Walks a finished project folder — files, notes, exported history — and proposes invoice lines for you to edit.
- **For:** Independents who bill after the work, not during it.
- **Why:** The source is what exists on disk, not a timer that was never started.
- **Build:** month

### Room Before the Call

- **Does:** Before a meeting, opens only your notes on that person and lists what you promised last time and what they asked for.
- **For:** Operators who keep relationships in files, not in a cloud CRM.
- **Why:** It will not go looking them up. If it is not in your notes, it is not in the brief.
- **Build:** month

### Half-Finished

- **Does:** Answers one question — "what was I in the middle of?" — from recently touched files and the last note.
- **For:** People who context-switch harder than they plan.
- **Why:** A single question with a single answer beats another dashboard.
- **Build:** weekend

### Decision Jar

- **Does:** When your notes say a decision was made, files the date, the choice, and the alternative you rejected.
- **For:** Founders and leads who relitigate last month.
- **Why:** The rejected option is the part everyone forgets, and it is the part that settles the argument.
- **Build:** month

## 2. Privacy Tools

Tools that protect the operator's own privacy, data, and devices. Nothing here watches anyone else.

### Exif Ash

- **Does:** Strips location, device identifiers, and serial-bearing metadata from a photo folder, and keeps a sealed local sidecar so you can restore.
- **For:** Anyone about to post or send pictures.
- **Why:** Removal without a memory of what was removed makes people afraid to clean the file.
- **Build:** weekend

### Paste Guard

- **Does:** On your own machine, warns when a password-shaped or key-shaped clipboard value is about to land in a browser or a chat window.
- **For:** Operators who copy secrets between tools all day.
- **Why:** The leak is usually a paste, not a breach, and the warning sits at the paste.
- **Build:** month

### Lend Mode

- **Does:** One switch for handing over your laptop in the room: guest session, no autofill, a lamp when the camera is live, session gone on exit.
- **For:** People who lend a machine to a guest, a kid, or a coworker for ten minutes.
- **Why:** "Be careful" is not a mode. A mode is a mode.
- **Build:** month

### Home Census

- **Does:** Names the devices on a network you administer and notes which of them spoke to the internet overnight.
- **For:** Households and tiny offices who want to know their own rooms.
- **Why:** The interesting object is the device you do not recognize, told in a sentence, not a port table.
- **Build:** month

### Quiet Downloads

- **Does:** In folders you choose, finds files that hold emails, phone numbers, or ID-like strings, and offers to move them into an encrypted drawer.
- **For:** People whose Downloads folder became a junk drawer of other people's data.
- **Why:** Privacy work is often mopping up what you accidentally kept.
- **Build:** month

### Link Bare

- **Does:** Rewrites a URL you are about to share so tracking parameters are gone, and shows the bare destination before you send it.
- **For:** Anyone who forwards links all day.
- **Why:** Most tracking rides along because the address was copied whole.
- **Build:** weekend

### Restore Drill

- **Does:** Once a week, pulls one random file from your own backup, confirms it opens, and writes a one-line diary entry.
- **For:** People who own backups they have never restored.
- **Why:** An untested backup is a rumor. A diary of opened files is evidence.
- **Build:** month

### Sensor Roll

- **Does:** One panel listing which apps on this machine currently hold the microphone or camera, with revoke beside each name.
- **For:** Anyone who wants the sensor story without a tour of settings.
- **Why:** The permission you granted in 2023 is still the story, and it should be visible in one place.
- **Build:** weekend

### Forgetful Profile

- **Does:** A browser profile that remembers only the sites you pin; every other site starts blank next launch.
- **For:** Research sessions that should not become a profile of you.
- **Why:** Memory should be opt-in per site, not a personality you accumulate by accident.
- **Build:** month

### Key Diary

- **Does:** A local record of which SSH keys and signed-in apps were used from this machine, so a login you do not remember stands out.
- **For:** People with more machines than habits.
- **Why:** The surprise is the signal. A quiet diary makes the surprise readable.
- **Build:** month

### Travel Seal

- **Does:** A pre-travel checklist for your laptop: which disks are encrypted, which accounts are signed out, which folders would sync from a strange network.
- **For:** People who carry a working machine across borders and hotels.
- **Why:** The useful moment is the night before, when you can still change the answer.
- **Build:** weekend

## 3. Offensive Security Tools

Lab concepts for a GUI-first, AI-orchestrated bench. Every one assumes the system is yours, or you hold a written authorization to test it. The ideas stop at the workbench: scope, map, notes, evidence, and the story of a fix. They are not attack write-ups.

### Scope Lantern

- **Does:** A gate on the lab UI: an action runs only when the host is on the written list of systems you own or are authorized to test.
- **For:** Testers whose staging and production sit too close together.
- **Why:** The safety check is the interface, so "I didn't notice the host" is harder to do.
- **Build:** weekend

### Range Cartographer

- **Does:** You describe your lab — machines, roles, who trusts whom — and it draws a living map that updates when you add a machine you declared.
- **For:** People who test stacks they built.
- **Why:** The map is hand-scoped. It does not go looking past the lab you named.
- **Build:** month

### Assumption Ledger

- **Does:** During a test of a system you own, every "I believe this is internal" becomes a card you confirm or retire before the report can close.
- **For:** Solo testers who skip the boring check.
- **Why:** Findings sit downstream of assumptions, and assumptions are usually unwritten.
- **Build:** weekend

### Surface Sketch

- **Does:** Points at a repository you own, draws routes, jobs, and webhooks as a picture, and lets an assistant ask review questions you still have to answer.
- **For:** App owners doing their own pass before a release.
- **Why:** A picture plus a question beats a dump of scanner rows you will not read.
- **Build:** month

### Finding Narrator

- **Does:** Turns lab notes you already wrote into two pages: a plain-language story for the owner, and a technical appendix that stays in the appendix.
- **For:** Operators who can find an issue and hate writing the report.
- **Why:** The owner page is required to stay free of reproduction detail. The story is the fix, not the trick.
- **Build:** month

### Fix Witness

- **Does:** After you patch your own staging app, replays a session you recorded in that lab and shows whether the same path still fails.
- **For:** Builders who want a finding to stay dead.
- **Why:** The artifact is a before-and-after of your own staging, attached to the ticket.
- **Build:** quarter

### Duplicate Court

- **Does:** Loads results you already collected in an authorized lab and clusters repeats so you keep one representative and dismiss the family.
- **For:** Anyone staring at the same finding fifty times.
- **Why:** Triage is the whole product. Collecting more rows is not.
- **Build:** month

### Tabletop Clock

- **Does:** In a GUI timeline, an assistant plays the passing minutes and a confused teammate while you practice a response for your own environment.
- **For:** Small teams who have never drilled.
- **Why:** Nothing touches a live system. The lab is the conversation, and the transcript is the lesson.
- **Build:** quarter

### Throwaway Bench

- **Does:** Issues short-lived test accounts inside a lab you control, and expires them when you mark the engagement over.
- **For:** Testers who should not reuse a personal login in a lab.
- **Why:** A lab identity with a funeral is safer than a shared password with a nickname.
- **Build:** month

### Trust Diff

- **Does:** Compares two builds of your own service and highlights new routes, new fields, and authorization checks that disappeared.
- **For:** Owners who ship every week.
- **Why:** The alert is "your app changed," which you can verify by reading the diff you already own.
- **Build:** month

### Purple Desk

- **Does:** Left pane, the feature you just put on staging you own; right pane, the abuse cases you wrote for it, with pass, fail, or not-tested recorded beside each.
- **For:** Product engineers who want the review in the same window as the change.
- **Why:** The assistant may only record the cases you supplied. It does not invent a procedure.
- **Build:** quarter

### Evidence Shelf

- **Does:** Pins each lab finding to the screenshot, captured request, and note you attached, and will not mark it confirmed without one of them.
- **For:** People whose current report is a chat log.
- **Why:** A finding you cannot show is a feeling. The shelf makes "show it" the default.
- **Build:** month
